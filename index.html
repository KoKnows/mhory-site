The story of where you are.

An app for iPhone and Android that reads the public record for the place you're standing in and has an AI write it up: what was here, when, and how we know. Ask it anything, and it answers from the sources, which it shows you.

When you'd open it

Moments that come with a question

The restaurant has a forty-minute wait, in a town you'd never heard of until the car needed a break.

You open Mhory on the bench outside. In a few seconds it has read the public record for the half-mile around you and written the short version: the mill the town grew up around, the rail line that made it, the fire that took the hotel in 1911 and the one across the street that replaced it. The one you're waiting outside of.

Your daughter asks what the green plaque on the corner says, and you have no idea.

The plaque is on the map, pinned, two doors down. Tapping it gives you the listing it came from and the date it was granted. She asks who lived there. You type that into Ask, and the answer names the family, with a link to the article it got that from, so you can tell her and then read the rest yourself.

The hotel is in an old building, and the lobby feels like it has a past it isn't mentioning.

It was a bank. Then, for thirty years, a department store. The timeline runs down from the renovation last decade to the land grant that put a street here in the first place, each entry with its source beside it. You go to bed knowing which floor held the vault.

You've walked this block for six years. You've never once looked up.

Set the radius to a mile and let the map fill in. There's a listed building on your own street that the registry describes in a paragraph, a church older than the city's charter, and the spot where the streetcar line turned. You leave a mark on the timeline at the corner, for yourself, the way you'd dog-ear a page.

What's doing the reading

An AI that only says what the record says

For wherever you are, Mhory collects what has been published about that ground: geotagged Wikipedia articles, dated facts from Wikidata, historic and heritage features in OpenStreetMap, and listings from the US National Register of Historic Places and Historic England. Each item keeps its source.

That collection, and nothing else, goes to Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, which writes the overview, divides the history into eras, and picks out the moments that mattered. Every sentence it writes is tied to a numbered source in the collection. If it can't tie a claim to one, the claim is dropped before you see it. The same rule applies when you use Ask: answers quote their sources, and when a short entry isn't enough, the AI fetches the full article and reads it first.

What you see is three views of the same reading: the written account with its timeline, the map with the places pinned around you, and Ask for whatever you want to know next.

Example

One street, read all the way down

Pudding Lane in the City of London is a short, plain street of offices. This is its timeline, written by hand from the same records Mhory reads, in the shape the app gives it.

  1. c. AD 47

    Londinium is founded

    The Romans establish a port on this stretch of the north bank. The lane runs down toward the site of their quays.

  2. 2 Sept 1666

    The Great Fire begins

    Fire breaks out in Thomas Farriner's bakehouse on Pudding Lane in the early hours. Over four days it destroys most of the medieval city, including some 13,000 houses, 87 parish churches and St Paul's Cathedral.

  3. 1677

    The Monument is completed

    A 202-foot column commemorating the fire. Its height equals the distance from its base to the site of the bakery where the fire started.

  4. 29 Dec 1940

    The Blitz

    The heaviest incendiary raid of the war sets the City alight in what is later called the Second Great Fire of London. St Paul's survives.

  5. Now

    Your mark

    If you sign the timeline here, your mark appears at this point, in brass, alongside the recorded past.

Your trail

Signing the timeline

At any place, you can leave a mark with an optional note. Marks appear on the timeline and the map next to the historical entries, and they're kept on your phone only. Nothing you write is uploaded.

Accounts and cost

Free to start

History, the timeline and the map work without an account. The AI summary and Ask need you to sign in with Apple or Google. Signing in is what lets Mhory provide the AI features at no setup cost to you. New accounts get a free monthly allowance of summaries and chat messages. Mhory Plus, a subscription through the App Store or Google Play billed monthly or yearly, raises those limits well beyond normal use.

Sources and privacy

Where the history comes from, and where your data doesn't go

Mhory reads Wikipedia and Wikidata (Wikimedia Foundation), OpenStreetMap and Nominatim (OpenStreetMap Foundation), the National Register of Historic Places (US National Park Service) and the National Heritage List for England (Historic England). Each receives the coordinates needed to answer a request and nothing about you.

Your location is used while the app is open and isn't stored by us. There are no advertising or analytics trackers. The full details are in the Privacy Policy.
